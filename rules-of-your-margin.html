Rules of Your Margin

A short manual for inhabitants.

I · Three kinds of stars

A book is a star with a name. You read it; it remains.

A concept is a place where ideas meet. It glows brighter when more books touch it.

A thought is your own — planted by you, kept by you. It is marked with ✦.

II · How stars appear

Books arrive when you read them.

Concepts arrive when Marginalia notices them — extracted from the books you've read, or, more quietly, from the sentences you have underlined more than once.

Thoughts arrive when you write them in the line below the sky.

III · Candidate stars · the ghosts

When Marginalia sees a phrase repeating in your notes, it appears in your sky as a dim, unnamed point. Hover to read its provenance.

You may welcome it (✓) — it joins the universe. Or dismiss it (✕) — it fades over a count of five.

Marginalia proposes; you dispose.

IV · Lines

Auto-lines are drawn by Marginalia when a book contains a concept. They are faint. They are not yours.

User-lines are drawn by you — by clicking one star, then another. They are plum, dashed, named. They are the actual record of your thinking.

If you draw the same line twice, it thickens. The universe remembers your repetitions; they are not redundant, they are convictions.

V · The archetype

Each week, Marginalia names the shape your reading is making. The Cartographer if you cross disciplines. The Close Reader if you return to one author. The One Who Pauses if you have been gone.

It is not a score. It is a noticing.

VI · The replay

At any moment you may rewind. The universe will assemble itself from Day One to today. Books first. Concepts second. Your own thoughts third. Your own lines last — because you are last. That is how Marginalia sees it.

VII · Decay, not deletion

Stars and lines you have not touched in six months will dim, but they will not disappear. This is a library, not a productivity tool. Some readings wait.

VIII · Anniversary

On the day you first opened LOOMUS, a postcard arrives. It is your sky as it stood twelve months earlier, beside your sky as it stands today. Marginalia signs it.

This is not a notebook. It is a margin — the strip beside the text where readers, for a thousand years, have written what only they would understand. Your margin is for you. We are only keeping it tidy. — M.
